Programme Case Study

Psychological & Social Support Programme for Female Inmates

A first-of-its-kind rehabilitation initiative conceived, coordinated, and led by Captain Aisha Hassan Alsumaim — in strategic partnership with the Royal Charity Organisation for Humanitarian Works — to deliver structured psychological and social support to female inmates at the Reform, Rehabilitation & Pretrial Detention Centre for Women in Bahrain.

Launched Nov 2025 20 inmates · 2 post-release 80%+ attendance
20 Inmates in Cohort
2 Post-Release Cases
6 Counselling Pillars
80%+ Sustained Attendance
01

Programme Overview

Background

Female inmates at the Reform, Rehabilitation & Pretrial Detention Centre for Women in Bahrain needed a structured, evidence-based pathway for psychological recovery and social reintegration — beyond what standard detention routines could provide.

Challenge

No dedicated psychological and social support programme existed for female inmates. Welfare programming required inter-agency coordination, individualised needs assessment, and continuity of care extending beyond release — capabilities no single entity could deliver alone.

Solution

Designed and led a partnership programme with the Royal Charity Organisation, combining weekly in-centre counselling, individualised psychological and social assessments, complementary wellbeing activities, and structured post-release aftercare — built around six clearly defined therapeutic pillars.

Impact

Achieved and sustained above 80% session attendance across a cohort of 20 inmates, with measurable improvements in psychological wellbeing, self-confidence, and family relationships — and continuity of care extended to 2 cases post-release.

02

Six Counselling Pillars

01

Self-Acceptance

Helping participants develop a healthy self-image and acknowledge their worth without judgment — a foundational step toward sustained behavioural change.

02

Social Insight

Building awareness of social dynamics and the participant's role within their family and community, supporting healthier interpersonal choices.

03

Stress Management

Equipping participants with practical techniques to recognise, process, and regulate stress in custody and after release.

04

Behavioural Change

Structured work on identifying triggers, replacing harmful patterns, and reinforcing positive routines through supervised responsibility.

05

Family Support

Strengthening family ties and communication channels — recognised as a key predictor of successful reintegration and reduced recidivism.

06

Future Planning

Guiding participants to articulate realistic post-release goals and identify concrete next steps, with linkage to aftercare services.

Programme Timeline

2024
Coordination Initiated

Initial strategic coordination with the Royal Charity Organisation for Humanitarian Works — defining scope, governance, and joint delivery model.

Nov 2025
Full Implementation Launched

Programme officially launched with weekly in-centre counselling sessions and a cohort of 20 selected inmates based on individualised needs assessments.

Ongoing
Continuity of Care Post-Release

Extended structured support to 2 cases following release — linking them to the Royal Charity Organisation's 'Ma'akum' aftercare initiative.

Next Phase
Aftercare Recommendations

Authored programme recommendations to scale structured aftercare services — supporting sustainable reintegration and reduced recidivism.

04

Outcomes & Impact

80%+ attendance sustained across the cohort throughout the programme.

Measurable wellbeing gains in psychological health, self-confidence, and family relationships.

Cross-sector partnership established with a national humanitarian organisation.

Post-release continuity delivered to 2 cases via the Ma'akum aftercare initiative.

Life-skills development through supervised responsibility in fitness, workshops, and engagement activities.

Scalable aftercare model documented for future expansion across additional cases.
